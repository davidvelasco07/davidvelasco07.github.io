Welcome

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the School of Natural Sciences at the Institute for Advanced Study, and a Visiting Research Collaborator in the Department of Astrophysical Sciences at Princeton University.

My research is on numerical methods for astrophysical fluid dynamics. I am especially interested in high-order methods, which reach a given accuracy with far fewer degrees of freedom than the second-order schemes most codes rely on, and in making them robust enough for real astrophysical flows, with their shocks, steep gradients and scales that cannot be fully resolved. I am equally interested in performance: how these methods are implemented and optimized so that they run efficiently on modern supercomputers, GPUs in particular. On the application side, my background is in planet formation and planet–disc interaction.

Previously: postdoctoral researcher at Princeton University (2022–2025) and at the Institute for Computational Science, University of Zurich (2019–2022). PhD in Computational Modelling and Scientific Computing, UAEM, Cuernavaca, Mexico (2019).

Research

High-order methods and their robustness

High-order methods reach a given accuracy with far fewer degrees of freedom than second-order schemes, but at shocks and other discontinuities they produce spurious oscillations, which can drive the density or the pressure negative. I work on the Spectral Difference method, a discontinuous finite-element scheme of arbitrary order, combined with a posteriori limiting: every update is tested against numerical and physical admissibility criteria, and in the cells that fail, the high-order fluxes are replaced by those of a second-order MUSCL–Hancock scheme defined on the sub-cells of each element. The solution keeps its high order where the flow is smooth and remains stable at discontinuities. With Romain Teyssier and Maria Han Veiga, we have developed the method for the Euler equations, for low Mach number flows such as stellar convection, and for the Navier–Stokes equations.

Multigrid solvers for self-gravity

Self-gravity requires the solution of Poisson's equation for the gravitational potential at every time step. Relaxation methods such as Jacobi or Gauss–Seidel solve it with simple local updates, but they only damp the error efficiently at the scale of the mesh spacing: its smooth, long-wavelength part decays slowly, and the number of iterations needed to converge grows with the resolution. Multigrid applies the same relaxation on a hierarchy of coarser meshes, where those long wavelengths become short and are damped quickly, so that a converged solution is reached in a few cycles, whatever the resolution. I work on the multigrid self-gravity solver of AthenaK, on adaptively refined meshes and on GPUs: the interpolation at the boundaries between refinement levels, which has to be conservative for orbits not to drift, and the performance of the solver on large GPU clusters. It is now used in work on tidal disruption events and on black holes inside massive stars.

GPU codes at scale

I write and optimize simulation codes for GPUs: spd_K, a Kokkos implementation of the Spectral Difference method, the nested-mesh version of FARGO3D, and contributions to AthenaK and RAMSES. On these machines most of the performance is decided by how a code uses the hardware rather than by the number of operations it performs: the memory access pattern and the data locality of its kernels, the number of kernels launched per step, and the way ghost zones are exchanged between mesh blocks and between ranks.

Planets in gas and dust discs

A planet embedded in a disc heats the gas around it, and that heat changes the force the gas exerts back. With Frédéric Masset I studied these thermal forces, the dynamical friction on luminous bodies, and how accretion of pebbles can drive a low-mass planet onto an eccentric orbit, using nested meshes to resolve scales far below the planet's Hill radius.

Publications

First-author papers

  • Spectral Difference method with a posteriori limiting: III – Navier–Stokes equations with arbitrary high-order accuracy

    D. A. Velasco-Romero & R. Teyssier · submitted to MNRAS (2026)

    An arbitrarily high-order treatment of the Laplacian for the Spectral Difference method. The scheme resolves viscous and diffusive scales at lower resolution than low-order methods and stays stable when those scales are under-resolved.

    arXiv:2604.07339

  • Spectral Difference method with a posteriori limiting: II – Application to low Mach number flows

    D. A. Velasco-Romero & R. Teyssier · MNRAS 537, 2387 (2025)

    Stellar convection is slow compared with sound, and most shock-capturing schemes drown it in numerical diffusion. We combine a low-Mach correction of the HLLC Riemann solver with a well-balanced formulation to evolve small perturbations on top of a steep hydrostatic equilibrium.

    Journal · arXiv:2405.11063

  • Dust rings trap protoplanets on eccentric orbits and get consumed by them

    D. A. Velasco-Romero, F. S. Masset, A. Morbidelli, P. Benítez-Llambay, L. Krapp & E. Lega · MNRAS (2024)

    Three-dimensional two-fluid simulations with nested meshes of protoplanets of 0.1 to 8 Earth masses near a dusty ring. Two stable eccentric orbits keep the planet close to the ring, halt its migration and let it accrete the dust; on the outer one, a strong thermal force at each passage through the ring produces bursts of accretion.

    Journal · arXiv:2407.16546

  • Spectral Difference method with a posteriori limiting: Application to the Euler equations in one and two space dimensions

    D. A. Velasco-Romero, M. Han Veiga & R. Teyssier · MNRAS (2023)

    An arbitrarily high-order Spectral Difference method with a second-order finite-volume scheme as fallback. Troubled cells are detected after each update and only their fluxes are replaced, which gives robust shock capturing without giving up the accuracy of the smooth flow.

    Journal · arXiv:2209.06597 · Interactive explainer

  • Eccentricity driving of pebble accreting low-mass planets

    D. A. Velasco-Romero, F. S. Masset & R. Teyssier · MNRAS 509, 5622 (2022)

    Simulations of a freely moving, luminous planet in a thermally diffusive disc with a nested-mesh version of FARGO3D. The resolution reached gives converged values of the eccentricity a low-mass planet attains as a function of its luminosity.

    Journal · arXiv:2111.11070

  • Dynamical friction with radiative feedback – II. High-resolution study of the subsonic regime

    D. A. Velasco-Romero & F. S. Masset · MNRAS 495, 2063 (2020)

    Simulations that resolve the Bondi radius of a perturber moving through uniform gas. For small masses the net force agrees with linear theory; at larger masses the thermal forces fall below it.

    Journal · arXiv:2004.13422

  • Numerical study of dynamical friction with thermal effects – I. Comparison to linear theory

    D. A. Velasco-Romero & F. S. Masset · MNRAS 483, 4383 (2019)

    How heat changes the drag on a massive body moving through gas, with and without its own luminosity. The range of scales involved required nested meshes, implemented in the GPU version of FARGO3D.

    Journal · arXiv:1812.03191

  • Planet–disc interactions with Discontinuous Galerkin methods using GPUs

    D. A. Velasco-Romero, M. Han Veiga, R. Teyssier & F. S. Masset · MNRAS (2018)

    A two-dimensional, high-order Discontinuous Galerkin code that runs on several GPUs, compared with FARGO3D on a planet–disc problem through the numerical viscosity inferred from the torque on the planet. Fifth-order schemes reach numerical viscosities of a few 10−8 r2Ω.

    Journal · arXiv:1805.01443

Software

  • spd Spectral Difference method with a posteriori limiting in Python (NumPy/CuPy, MPI). The reference code of the paper series. Main developer.
  • spd_K The same method in C++ with Kokkos, for CPUs and GPUs. Main developer.
  • AthenaK Performance-portable AMR code for astrophysical (GR)MHD. Contributor: multigrid self-gravity, communication and performance on GPU systems.
  • FARGO3D + nested meshes Nested meshes for FARGO3D on GPUs. Main developer.
